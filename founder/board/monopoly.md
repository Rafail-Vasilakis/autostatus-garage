# Monopoly lens
Score: 7/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Starts with an unassailable micro-monopoly (Thiel's Law):** The business does not attempt to conquer global auto software. It starts with a single workshop (the father's shop), expands to the immediate municipal neighborhood, and captures the independent Greek repair market where global competitors cannot localize.
- **10x Improvement on Bay Clearance Latency:** Waiting for a customer to answer a phone call to approve a €90 brake rotor replacement currently stalls a lift for 1 to 3 hours. Replacing phone tag with a 1-tap Viber photo approval clears the lift 10x faster, directly increasing the shop's daily revenue capacity.
- **Incumbent Blindspot:** Existing garage software vendors in Greece and Southern Europe sell 15-year-old on-premise Windows desktop accounting programs. They are slow, rigid, and completely detached from the customer's mobile chat reality.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The Distribution Trap:** Mechanics do not read SaaS blogs, do not click Facebook software ads, and do not attend tech webinars. If the founder relies on digital marketing, Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) will be fatal. Distribution requires in-person boots-on-the-ground visits or strategic partnerships with auto parts distributors who already have daily delivery drivers visiting every workshop.
2. **Defensibility against legacy ERP copycats:** Once the concept proves profitable, what prevents local automotive ERP providers (or SMS marketing agencies) from adding a "Send WhatsApp Status" plugin to their existing billing software? The defensibility must come from customer-mechanic two-sided lock-in and ease-of-use, not just the API.
3. **High churn from non-tech-savvy operators:** Many independent mechanics are in their 40s to 60s and resist digital screens. If training takes more than 10 minutes, or if the interface requires manual typing rather than camera/voice inputs, retention will plummet after the free pilot ends.

## The questions I would ask the founder
1. What is your proprietary distribution channel to reach 50 garages without spending thousands of euros on travel or cold calling? Can local spare parts distributors distribute this for you?
2. What is your "secret" that other software engineers don't know? (Is it the psychological script of how a mechanic talks to a driver, or the exact workflow on the lift?)
3. How do you create switching costs so that once a shop has 200 customer vehicle histories in your system, they can never leave?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Direct In-Person Distribution Playbook:** The founder must personally sign up 5 paying workshops (outside of their father's shop) through direct walk-ins or warm mechanic referrals to prove a repeatable sales script.
2. **Data Lock-in & Annual Retention Engine:** The tool must store complete service histories and automatically trigger the 11-month service reminder. Once a mechanic sees repeat customers returning because of automated reminders, the churn drops to near zero.
3. **Channel Partnership Pilot:** The founder must interview at least 1 local auto parts distributor or motor oil wholesaler about a referral or bundle partnership.
