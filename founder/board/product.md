# Product lens
Score: 9/10 | Vote: FUND

## What is strong
- **Ruthless Subtraction & Radical Focus:** The product rejects 90% of what traditional enterprise garage software tries to build. It has no complex inventory modules, no double-entry accounting ledgers, and no multi-warehouse tracking. It does ONE thing with surgical excellence: **bridges the communication gulf between the hydraulic lift and the car owner's smartphone.**
- **Designed for the Reality of Dirty Hands:** Building large, high-contrast touch targets that can be tapped with greasy knuckles, work gloves, or while leaning into an engine bay shows genuine craft and empathy for the user's physical environment.
- **Transformational Customer Experience:** From the car owner's perspective, dropping off a car at an independent garage has historically been an experience filled with anxiety and suspicion ("Are they overcharging me?"). Receiving a clean, timestamped photo on Viber showing the exact broken part with an upfront price turns a traditionally opaque, adversarial interaction into an experience of transparency, trust, and delight.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Intake Friction Creep:** If logging an incoming vehicle requires entering 10 fields (VIN, engine code, mileage, insurance info, full address), the mechanic will abandon it in 48 hours. Intake must be strictly under 15 seconds: License Plate, Name, Phone Number, Done.
2. **Feature Bloat:** The inevitable temptation from early customers asking: "Can it also track my oil barrel inventory?", "Can it print my tax receipts?", "Can it manage employee payroll?". Succumbing to feature creep will destroy the clean, ultra-fast 3-button mobile experience.
3. **Seam Failures in Mobile Deliverability:** If the customer's phone doesn't have Viber installed, or if the WhatsApp template is blocked by Meta, or if the photo takes 30 seconds to upload over spotty garage Wi-Fi, the seamless magic breaks. Fallbacks (SMS fallback, instant local compression of photos) must be rock solid.

## The questions I would ask the founder
1. Can an incoming car be logged in literally under 15 seconds by taking a photo of the license plate with automated OCR?
2. How does the interface look and behave on a cheap Android phone with a cracked, grease-stained screen under harsh garage neon lights?
3. What is the single feature you will refuse to build no matter how many users ask for it?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
*(Member votes FUND unconditionally, but notes key craft requirements:)*
1. **The 15-Second Intake Benchmark:** Vehicle intake must never exceed 3 taps or 15 seconds. If OCR (license plate recognition) can make it 1 tap, prioritize it.
2. **Instant Media Compression:** Photos taken on the phone must be compressed on-device before uploading so that weak 3G/Wi-Fi in basement repair shops never hangs the interface.
3. **Zero Software Training Required:** The mechanic UI must be so self-evident that an apprentice or veteran mechanic can use it on Day 1 without reading a manual or watching a tutorial.
